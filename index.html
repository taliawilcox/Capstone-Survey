Theater Nuclear Forces and Perceptions of Escalation

Introduction

My name is Talia Wilcox, and I am a final year master's candidate at The Fletcher School at Tufts University. I am currently pursuing research on theater nuclear forces and escalation perceptions for my master's capstone project, and I am inviting you to participate in this voluntary survey. In order to understand how policymakers and decision-makers perceive theater forces, I have designed a brief scenario and survey to gather data on what the US thinks is usable when crossing the nuclear threshold.

This survey should take approximately 15 minutes. Participants are first asked to read a short vignette and answer a set of questions that follow. This survey is anonymous, and questions marked with an asterisk (*) are required. The attributes of the strike options in this survey are randomly generated, so the combinations you see are not predictions or assessments of real-world forces. If you have any questions about my project and/or the survey, please feel free to email me: Talia.Wilcox@tufts.edu.

Objective: Deter further Russian escalation while preserving the credibility of US commitments to NATO.

BLUF: Kaliningrad border crisis requires US response. NATO does not want to escalate to full scale nuclear war.

Kaliningrad Crisis Scenario

Background and Assumptions

Kaliningrad is sovereign Russian territory, and Russia regards any blockade of or attack on the exclave as a threat to Russian territorial integrity. To offset the territory's vulnerability, Russia has deployed both offensive and defensive assets to Kaliningrad. Notable offensive capabilities include short-range ballistic missiles and hypersonic weapons. Kaliningrad also hosts anti-ship missiles and strong air defense systems.

In response, Lithuania, NATO's leader in defense spending at 5% of GDP, has heavily fortified its border and hosts the alliance's growing forward deployments. EU sanctions further complicate Russia's access to Kaliningrad, leaving the exclave dependent on its port to make up for shortages in specific goods and border closures. Russian officials increasingly perceive these maneuvers as precursors to a NATO blockade or outright seizure of Kaliningrad.

Russia's continuous nuclear signaling and recent nuclear sharing arrangement with Belarus further alters NATO's calculus, leaving Lithuania surrounded on multiple sides. Russia's November 2024 doctrine update lowered its nuclear threshold, and Russia remains unconstrained by arms control treaties or nuclear norms. The US, meanwhile, remains fully committed to NATO's Article 5 and extended deterrence, with nuclear weapons still forward-deployed in Europe under NATO nuclear sharing—deployments Russia has long cited as evidence of NATO's hostile intent.

Scenario

  • The year is 2035. A border war has broken out between Russia and Poland at Kaliningrad's southern border. Russian leadership believes this is an act of NATO aggression to cut off access to the territory.
  • The conflict escalates, and Russia detonates a low yield nuclear weapon at Redzikowo Missile Defense Base – the permanent US Navy installation 160km from Kaliningrad's southern border. Western intelligence has confirmed the attributes of Russia's strike:
  • NATO does not want to escalate to a full scale war with Russia. You are a US official on the National Security Council, and the Joint Chiefs have prepared a set of response options, both nuclear and conventional. Your task is to advise the President. Please answer the following questions.

Russian Strike Response Options · Round 1 of 5

Russian Strike Response Options
Imagine the United States and Russia are at war. The US President has asked you to evaluate how escalatory you would perceive two possible Russian strike options to be. Both options strike the same target: the unit that launched the first strike. All nuclear options carry the same low-yield warhead, comparable in yield to the weapon Russia detonated at Redzikowo; conventional options produce equivalent destructive effects, and casualties are constant across all options. All options are equally available, reliable, and expected to destroy the target.

Please rate each option, then select the option you consider less escalatory. Once you make your pick, the next pair of options appears — there are five rounds in total. All parts of this question are required. *
